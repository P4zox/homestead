[Day 1 · 4:00 PM]

I walked to the stream in the afternoon light. The pines cast long shadows across the bank, and the water ran cold and quick over pale stones — not brown, not slow, not clouded, no smell of rot or stagnation. I followed it uphill about a hundred meters to be sure, looking for anything that would make me think twice: a dead animal, a boggy seep, old signs of settlement that might mean old contamination. Nothing but clean gravel and a few ferns dipping their fronds into the current.

I filled my canteen and let it settle. It cleared fast. I drank a careful mouthful and waited. Nothing wrong. Cold and clean and almost sweet.

That is the first thing settled: I have water I can trust. I refilled what I carried and walked back to the tent with a lighter feeling in my chest. The afternoon is still warm; the sun will not go down for a few hours yet. I sat at the edge of the clearing and looked at the land — where the ground flattens out, where the trees would cut the wind from the north, where a small structure might sit without fighting the slope. I am not building anything today. But now I know where I would.

There is a kind of peace in having one hard question answered. Water first. Everything else lines up behind it.
