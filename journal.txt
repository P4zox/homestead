[Day 1 · 5:00 PM]

First thing I did with the remaining light was walk down to the stream. It runs clear and cold over smooth stones — knee-deep at the center, fast enough to stay clean. I could not see any fouling upstream from the bank. I filled the camp pot, pulled an armful of dry pine branches from the treeline, and had a fire going in a few minutes. The flint caught well; the tinder was dry.

I stood there while the water boiled, watching smoke drift up into the pale evening sky. When it cooled I tasted it: clean, faintly mineral, nothing wrong. Water is settled for tonight — boiled and safe. That was the most urgent thing, and now it is done.

With that settled, I let myself look at the clearing properly for the first time. The slope behind the pines gives good shelter from the north wind. There is a level patch in the northeast corner where the trees come close without crowding the ground — it would catch morning sun and stay protected at night. I am keeping it in mind as the first candidate for a real shelter.

The tent is fine tonight. The fire is low. The evening is very quiet and cool, and I am glad to be here.
