[Day 1 · 6:00 PM]

The light is going amber and the pines are throwing long shadows across the clearing. I have been here since morning, just standing and getting the lay of things — where the slope runs, which way the wind comes from, what the stream sounds like when I stop moving.

I went down to the water an hour or so before dusk. Knelt at the bank and looked at it a long time before I touched it. It runs clear over dark gravel, cold enough that my hand ached after a moment. I checked upstream as far as I could see — no discoloration, no smell, no film on the surface. Still, I scooped a pot full and carried it back to the tent, lit a fire with the flint, and boiled it until it rolled hard. When it cooled I drank. Clean. Faintly mineral, the way good mountain water does.

So: water I trust. That is the first solid thing.

The evening feels steadier for it. I am sitting outside the tent now with the empty pot beside me, watching the treeline go dark by degrees. The birch at the north edge of the clearing — there is a small rise there, a natural windbreak, and the ground looks higher and drier than the rest. That is where I will look for a shelter site tomorrow when the light is good.

One thing. That is enough for a first evening.
